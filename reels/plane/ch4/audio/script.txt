Phase four. Descent. Quick guess. When does the plane start coming down? Ten kilometres before the airport? Fifty? Try two hundred. Pilots use a simple rule. Three nautical miles for every thousand feet. Thirty five thousand feet, times three, is about a hundred nautical miles. That's around two hundred kilometres. Simple maths, smooth landing. Now, the sky near a big airport is busy. So air traffic control lines everyone up, like a queue at a railway ticket counter. Except here, nobody is allowed to cut the line. Not even uncle. Closer in, an invisible radio beam called ILS draws a perfect three degree slope down to the runway. The plane just slides down it, like a marble on a ramp. Flaps slide out of the wings, for more lift at low speed. And then. Landing gear down. Clunk. That noise? Totally normal. Uncle, sit down.
