Phase two. The climb. And now, the question your brain has been too polite to ask. How does a seventy tonne thing stay up? Magic? Prayers? Nope. Air. A wing is shaped and tilted so it shoves air downwards. Push air down, and the air pushes the wing up. Newton said so. That's lift. Try it yourself. Next time you're in a car, in the passenger seat, put your hand out the window and tilt it a little. Whoosh. Your hand wants to fly. Congratulations, you are a wing. More speed, more lift. And the speed comes from these. The engines. Quick guess. What pushes the plane more? The fire in the middle, or the plain air around it? Most people say fire. Nope. A giant fan sucks in air. A little goes into the core, gets squeezed, mixed with fuel and burned, and that spins the turbines, which spin the fan. But most of the air just flows around the core, and that bypass air makes about eighty percent of the push. The fan is the hero. The fire is just its gym. Meanwhile, the cabin pressure slowly drops, the air in your ears wants out, and pop. Swallow, yawn, or chew something. The uncle? Chewing. Aggressively. Next up, the cruise.
