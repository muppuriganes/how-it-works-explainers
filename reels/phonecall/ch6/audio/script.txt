On the other side, the packets reach your friend's operator, then the tower near them, and fly through the air to their phone. Here, everything happens in reverse. The codec unpacks each packet and rebuilds the numbers. A converter turns those numbers back into an electrical signal. That signal drives the speaker, which shakes the air, and your friend hears your voice. But networks aren't perfectly smooth. Packets can arrive a little early, a little late, or not at all. So the phone keeps a small waiting area called a jitter buffer. It holds packets for a split second, so it can play them in a steady rhythm. If a packet goes missing, the codec makes a smart guess to fill the gap. But when the network is bad and too many go missing, that's when you hear that robotic, broken voice.
