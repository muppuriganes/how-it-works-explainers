It all starts with sound. When you speak, your voice pushes the air, making tiny waves of pressure. Inside your phone's microphone, a thin layer shakes with those waves, and turns the shaking into an electrical signal. But phones don't send waves. They send numbers. So your phone measures that signal sixteen thousand times every second, and writes down each measurement as a number. This is called sampling. Sending all of those numbers would be wasteful, so a clever program called a codec squeezes them down. Instead of every number, it sends a short description of your voice, twenty milliseconds at a time. That's fifty little packets of voice, every single second. And they are tiny. A whole minute of talking is only about a hundred kilobytes. That's smaller than one photo.
