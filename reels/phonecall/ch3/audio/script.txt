Now those packets need to leave your phone. Your phone's antenna turns them into radio waves. You can't see them, but they're the same kind of energy as light, so they travel at three hundred thousand kilometres per second. They head to the nearest cell tower. Your city is divided into small areas called cells, and each cell has a tower. That's why it's called a cell phone. Thousands of phones share each tower at the same time. To keep them apart, the tower gives every phone its own tiny slices of frequency and time, like giving each car its own lane on a highway. And if you're talking while moving, say in a car, your call is quietly passed from one tower to the next. This is called a handover, and it happens so smoothly that you never notice.
