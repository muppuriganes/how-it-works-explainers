But wait. Before any voice can travel, the network has to find the person you're calling. When you press call, your phone first sends a request, a small digital message that says: I want to talk to this number. It goes to your operator's core network, the brain of the whole system. The network already knows you're a real customer, because your SIM card proved who you are when your phone switched on. Next, it reads the number. The country code tells it which country, and the rest tells it which operator. So your request is passed across to that operator. But how do they know where the phone is? Every phone that's switched on keeps telling the network which area it's in. So the operator knows where to look. It sends a message through every tower in that area: phone, you have a call. The phone hears it, wakes up, and starts ringing. On your side, you hear the ringing tone. And when your friend picks up, a path opens between the two phones. All of this usually takes just a few seconds.
