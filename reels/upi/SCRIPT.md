# How UPI Works: where your ₹10 actually goes

Narrator: Neerja (Indian English, expressive). About 5 to 5.5 minutes.

## 1. The ₹10 chai

You scan a QR code. You type ten rupees. You enter your PIN. Beep. Ten rupees received. And you walk away with your chai, feeling like a total tech genius. Cute. Here's the thing. In those two seconds, two banks, a bunch of computers, and one very serious referee just held an emergency meeting. About your chai. So today, we're crashing that meeting. Hi, I'm your host. And by the end of this video, you'll know exactly where your ten rupees went. And why, sometimes, it goes on a little vacation.

## 2. Meet the gang

First, meet the gang. There's you, holding a phone and a chai. There's your payment app, which, plot twist, does not actually keep your money. It's basically a very polite messenger. There's your bank, where your money actually lives. There's the chai wala's bank. And right in the middle, there's NPCI, the National Payments Corporation of India. Think of it as the referee. Nobody gets paid until the referee says so. Oh, and that QR code on the counter? It's just the chai wala's UPI ID, his name, and sometimes the amount, dressed up as a fancy square. Very fashionable.

## 3. The two-second journey

Now, press pay, and watch closely. Step one. Your app writes a little message. Who's paying, who's getting paid, and how much. It hands it to its partner bank, which passes it to the referee. Step two. The referee looks at the chai wala's UPI ID and asks, which bank is this guy with? Like a phone book, but for money. Step three. The referee goes to your bank and says, this person wants to pay ten rupees. Is that okay? Your bank says, show me the PIN. Step four. Your bank checks the PIN, checks your balance, which, let's be honest, is always a tense moment, and takes out ten rupees. Step five. The referee tells the chai wala's bank, add ten rupees. Step six. Everyone sends everyone a confirmation, and the little speaker shouts, ten rupees received. All of that, in about two seconds. Your chai hasn't even cooled down.

## 4. The PIN

Let's talk about your PIN. When you type it, your app can't actually read it. It's locked up right there on your phone, with a lock that only your bank can open. So the app is just carrying a sealed envelope it isn't allowed to peek into. Respect. And now, the most important thing in this entire video. You need your PIN to send money. You never, ever need it to receive money. So if a stranger says, enter your PIN to receive your prize, congratulations. The only prize is them, emptying your account.

## 5. Why is it free?

Now, the million rupee question. Who pays for all of this? When you swipe a card, the shop pays a small fee on every payment. But for regular UPI payments to shops? The fee is zero. The government decided that. The banks were, let's say, not thrilled. So the government gives them some support to keep the lights on. Which means your ten rupee chai payment is low key sponsored. You're welcome, chai wala.

## 6. Money debited, payment failed

And now, the scariest message in India. Money debited. Payment failed. What happened? Usually, your bank did take the money out, but somewhere after that, a confirmation got stuck. Maybe a server was slow. Maybe the network sneezed. So the system isn't sure the chai wala got paid. Don't panic. Your money isn't lost. It's just stuck in traffic. By the rules, it should come back to your account automatically by the next working day. And if the bank takes longer, the rules say it has to pay you a hundred rupees for every extra day. So technically, a slow bank could buy you ten chais.

## 7. The big picture

So, the next time you pay for chai, remember. An app, two banks, and a referee just had a two second meeting, all about you. Now multiply that by billions of payments, every single month. That's how a country of over a billion people buys its chai. Anyway, your chai's getting cold. Go drink it. And maybe tip the chai wala. Through UPI, obviously.
