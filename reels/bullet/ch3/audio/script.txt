Coach three. There's no engine up front. No diesel. No smoke. The power hangs above you. Twenty five thousand volts runs through a wire over the track. On the roof, a springy arm called a pantograph presses up against that wire, sliding along at three hundred kilometres an hour without letting go. And here's the trick. There's no single locomotive dragging everyone. Motors are spread across the coaches, so the whole train pushes together. Like a tug of war team where everyone is actually pulling.
